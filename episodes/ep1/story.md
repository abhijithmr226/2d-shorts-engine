## Script (~60 s, plain words, user 2026-11-05)
London has these tiny green huts,
and you're not allowed to sit down and eat in them.
Unless you drive a taxi.
It all started on a snowy night in 1875.
A newspaper man called George Armstrong needed a ride home.
The street was full of horse taxis.
But there was no driver on any of them.
They were all inside the pub, getting warm.
You see, the rule was simple. If your taxi was parked, you had to stay with it.
So when it was freezing, a driver had two choices.
Sit outside and freeze.
Or pay someone to watch his horse, and go to the pub.
Most of them went to the pub.
So Armstrong and some rich friends built them a little room, right next to their taxis.
Hot food, hot tea, and three rules.
No drinking. No gambling. No swearing.
But there was a problem. The huts sat on the road.
And nothing on the road could be bigger than a horse and cart.
So they built them exactly that big.
About twelve drivers, squeezed around one tiny table.
They built 61 of them.
13 are still standing today, and every single one is protected.
And if you don't drive a taxi? You can still buy a sandwich.
You just have to eat it outside.
